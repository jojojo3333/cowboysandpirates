# SETTING.md — Sol, 2100

A v0.3 document. It exists now so `data/` is organised by region from the start
and so the do-not-copy boundaries in §6 are settled before anyone writes content.

---

## 1. Premise

Seventy-four years from now. No aliens, no faster-than-light, no time travel.
Fusion torch drives make Sol crossable in weeks instead of years, which is the
only speculative technology the setting needs.

The Solar System is not an empire. **It is a gold rush in its second decade.**

Earth's governments and the Mars Compact still exist, still hold the largest
fleets on paper, and are both twenty years behind the money. The corporations
that financed expansion own the drives, the refineries, the water, and the
people. When they fight — and they fight constantly — nobody declares anything.
Ships die weekly. The filings call it a contract dispute.

**You are a smuggler with a small ship and six people who depend on you.**

## 2. Why 2100 specifically

The date does structural work and must not drift.

- **No Belter ethnicity yet.** Nobody has been *born* out there and lived a full
  life. Rockjacks are a trade, not a people. This is the single biggest
  separation from The Expanse and it is a consequence of the calendar, not a
  choice we have to keep defending.
- **Mars is not a superpower.** A few million people, enormous debt, and a
  navy that exists mostly in press releases.
- **Power armour is plausibly rare.** Nobody mass-produces exosuits during a
  gold rush. Five components scattered across Sol is a scavenger story, which
  is the story we are telling.
- **Everyone's grandparents are still on Earth.** Nobody is fighting for a
  homeland. They are fighting over a claim.
- **Transits take weeks.** A run is a voyage of months. This is why the crew
  know each other, why shares accumulate, and why losing Vela matters. It never
  needs simulating — it only needs to be true.

## 3. The powers

### The corporations (the actual governments)

Four. **Each owns something the crew physically cannot do without.** That is
where their weight comes from — not from being powerful in the abstract, but
from the player needing them at a specific moment.

Movement. Breathing. Dying. Digging.

Each also has a **signature behaviour the player can recognise in ten seconds**,
because a faction the player cannot identify by feel is a logo, not a faction.

---

#### SARN — drives and shipyards
*They own movement.*

Founded by Aleksander Sarn, who built the first practical torch drive in a
Nevada hangar with forty people and a lawsuit for every one of them. SARN did
not win the expansion. SARN **caused** it. Every ship in Sol, including yours,
burns on their patents.

Sarn died in 2094. **The company still runs on him.**

There is a model — trained on four decades of his memos, calls, decisions and
rejections — and the board consults it before every major move. Officially it is
a decision-support tool. Nobody at SARN calls it that. Nobody outside SARN is
certain whether the board follows it or hides behind it, and the distinction has
stopped mattering.

*It is not him. It is the pattern without the person, and it is running the
largest company in Sol.* Whether anyone at SARN finds that troubling is not
public information.

- **Signature behaviour:** fast, terse, technically superb. Contracts arrive
  fully specified with no explanation of purpose. They pay on time. They never
  answer a question they were not asked.
- **Toward you:** they will hire a smuggler without hesitation and without
  courtesy. You are a line item that closed.
- **Their gear is the best in the game and it is expensive.**

#### Vantar Combine — water, air, life support
*They own breathing.*

The oldest of the four and the only one that predates expansion — a terrestrial
utility conglomerate that bought the ice and never had to fight for anything
again. Every station past Mars runs on a Vantar contract.

They treat their people well. Genuinely well: good wages, real pensions,
families housed. They are also the reason four hundred thousand people cannot
leave the moon they work on, and they have never once had to say so.

**Vantar never threatens. It does not need to, and it knows the difference is
invisible from outside.**

- **Signature behaviour:** patient, courteous, generous opening terms. The terms
  compound. Every Vantar offer is genuinely good and every Vantar debt is
  permanent.
- **Toward you:** warm. Helpful. Interested in your route.
- **This is the sympathetic antagonist.** If the player never feels conflicted
  about Vantar, the corporate war is cardboard and this faction has failed.

#### Lethe Medical — medicine, trauma, and the clone patents
*They own dying.*

Named for the river of forgetting, decades before anyone thought about clones —
back when the company made anaesthetics and the name was a joke about pain.

**Lethe holds the pattern-printing patents. Your Clone Bay is licensed from
them.** Every clone your crew comes back in was printed on their process, under
their terms, and the licence is per-use.

They are smaller than the other three and more dangerous than any of them,
because everyone needs them and nobody can build an alternative in time.

- **Signature behaviour:** present at every disaster, first on scene, endlessly
  helpful. Their aid arrives with consent forms. They always want a sample.
- **Toward you:** solicitous. They would like to know how the Clone Bay is
  performing. They would like to know what came back.
- **Design hook:** the game's central mechanic has a landlord. Use it.

#### Aurex — extraction and refining
*They own digging, and they are coming apart.*

Twenty years ago Aurex was the largest employer in Sol. Then Vantar priced their
water, SARN priced their drives, and the ore price did what ore prices do. The
Ganymede division folded in 2098 and left four thousand crew unpaid, six hundred
million kilometres from another job.

**A collapsing corporation is more dangerous than a healthy one.** Unpaid
security. Seized assets. Standing orders nobody has the authority to countermand
and nobody has bothered to rescind.

- **Signature behaviour:** erratic. Some Aurex ships will help you. Some will
  shoot. Neither knows why, and neither has been paid since spring.
- **Toward you:** unpredictable, and the unpredictability is the point.
- Aurex is where the Unbonded come from, and some of them still fly the logo.

---

### How they stand with each other

- **SARN and Vantar** need each other and cannot stand each other. SARN moves
  the ships, Vantar keeps the crews alive. Neither can move against the other
  without stopping Sol.
- **Lethe** is smaller than both and answers to neither, because both need it.
  It sells to everyone and is trusted by no one.
- **Aurex is being eaten.** SARN took the shipyards, Vantar took the water
  rights, Lethe bought the medical division for almost nothing. What is left is
  the part nobody wanted, and it is armed.

### Sol Authority (Earth)
The old government. Largest fleet, smallest budget, forty minutes of light-lag
from anywhere that matters. Bureaucratic, occasionally principled, structurally
too slow. Enforcement is real near Luna and theoretical past Mars.

### The Mars Compact
A confederation of domes with a flag and a debt. Independent on paper. In
practice, three corporations hold its infrastructure leases. Proud about it.

### Rockjacks
Not a faction — a workforce. Claim crews, refinery hands, and haulers spread
across the Belt and the Jovian moons. No unified politics, no shared language,
strong local loyalties. Some claims are effectively free ports. Others are
company towns with the doors welded shut. **They are what the player mostly
is**, socially.

### The Unbonded
Pirates, but not by inclination. When Aurex's Ganymede division folded, four
thousand crew found themselves unpaid and 600 million kilometres from a job.
Some of them took their ships. **Occasionally sympathetic. Never safe.**

## 4. Starting origins

The player picks one at run start. Same ship class, different starting
reputation, different opening region. FTL's ship-unlock structure.

- **Luna Runner** — Earth-side smuggler working the Luna–LEO corridor.
  Starts near law, near money, near scrutiny.
- **Compact Freelancer** — Mars-side. Starts with Compact goodwill and
  corporate suspicion.
- Further origins as unlocks (v0.4+).

## 5. Geography

**Geography is fixed. Contents are procedural.** Ceres is always Ceres and
always between Mars and Jupiter. Who is there, what is happening, and which
nodes are reachable this run are rolled per run.

This is the pleasure FTL structurally cannot offer: by run ten a player knows
Ganymede is where the farms are and Eros is where you do not stop.

| Region | Nodes | Character |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| **Luna & Earth orbit** | ~8 | Law exists. Expensive. Everyone reads your registry |
| **Mars & moons** | ~10 | Phobos, Deimos, the dome cities. Boomtown money, no oversight |
| **The Belt** | ~20 | Ceres, Vesta, Pallas, Eros, Hygiea, Juno, Psyche + invented claims |
| **Jovian moons** | ~12 | Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto. Industrial and brutal |
| **Saturn** | ~8 | Titan, Enceladus. The edge. Endgame |

Roughly 58 real locations before inventing anything. **Invent freely** —
"Kirkwood Yards", "Hygiea Transfer", "Bosch Station" — because in 2100 nobody
knows what is actually out there.

Difficulty is distance from Earth. Every jump outward is further from help.

**Runs are outbound only in v1.0.** Round trips are a good idea in the wrong
version: a return leg means backtracking through cleared content or a second
procedural pass, and neither pays for itself yet. Keep as a run variant.

## 6. DO NOT COPY — hard boundaries

We take structure and tone from these shows. We take nothing identifiable.
**If a reviewer can name the source, it is a bug.**

### The Expanse
- **No alien artifact of any kind.** No protomolecule, no precursor tech, no
  ancient anything. This is the entire spine of The Expanse and the single
  easiest thing to accidentally reinvent. Our long-term quest is power armour:
  human-made, industrial, scavenged.
- **No Belter creole.** No constructed dialect, no "beltalowda"-style in-group
  slang, no hand-sign language. Rockjacks speak with trade jargon and regional
  accents.
- **No three-way Earth/Mars/Belt cold war.** Our conflict is corporate and hot.
- **No Belter body morphology** — no tall, thin, low-gravity-adapted physiology
  as an ethnic marker.
- **No named drive.** No Epstein equivalent with an inventor legend.
- **No OPA.** Rockjacks have no unified political movement. That is the point.

### Altered Carbon
- **No consciousness transfer. This one matters most, because we have a Clone
  Bay.** No cortical stacks, no resleeving, no digital human storage, no
  immortality-for-the-rich.
- **Our Clone Bay prints a body from a stored biological pattern. The mind does
  not travel.** The clone is a person who was never you, wearing your face,
  starting from nothing. **That is exactly why XP is lost** — and it is the
  clean mechanical opposite of resleeving, where the mind is what survives.
  Anyone who writes "upload", "backup", or "download" in clone-related text has
  broken the setting.
- No Meths. No aristocracy of the immortal.

### Firefly
- **No civil war between a central Alliance and defeated independents.** Smith
  lost nothing in a war; he is in debt.
- **No Reavers**, and nothing structurally equivalent — no faction of the
  inhuman, no horror at the edge of the map.
- **No psychically altered escapee** aboard the ship.
- **No bilingual swearing tic.**
- Take instead: the frontier register, the job that goes wrong, crew shares,
  a captain who is not a hero.

### Killjoys
- No bounty-warrant institution as the world's structure. No neutrality-enforcing
  guild. No planetary Quad, no Level Six.
- Take instead: the call-sign naming register and the brisk, dry banter.

### Dark Matter
- No amnesia premise. Everyone aboard knows who they are.
- No numbered crew.
- **No corporate war as a formally declared galactic conflict** with fleet
  engagements and a peace summit. Ours is undeclared, commercial, and denied.
- No single dominant corporation the others orbit. Ferrous Corp was the sun in
  Dark Matter's system; ours has four bodies of comparable mass and no centre.
- No corporation defined mainly by being the most ruthless. Ruthlessness is a
  weak differentiator and it is what makes megacorporations interchangeable.
- Take instead: corporations as *actual* governments, the crew as deniable
  contractors, and betrayal as ordinary business rather than a plot twist.

### The founder-led drive company
SARN draws on a recognisable real-world archetype — the founder-led aerospace
firm that genuinely built the technology and made the expansion possible. **The
archetype is fair game. The person is not.**

- No electric cars, no social platform, no tunnels, no rockets named after
  fiction.
- No living founder with a public persona. Sarn has been dead six years and the
  interesting thing about him is what replaced him.
- No personal Mars-colonisation mission. SARN sells drives. Where people go with
  them is not their concern and never was.
- If a player can name the real person, rewrite the paragraph.

## 7. Naming rules

- **Places:** real Solar System bodies wherever one exists. Invented stations
  get plain industrial names — a founder, a function, a company.
- **People:** two syllables where possible, hard consonants, spellable on first
  hearing, survives being shouted across a burning room.
- **Corporations:** short, pronounceable, unglamorous. Companies name themselves
  after founders and mergers, not concepts.
- **Currency:** always "sats". Never the brand name it descends from.
- **No invented slang without a reason.** Every constructed dialect in sci-fi
  is measured against The Expanse's and loses.

## 8. What the player is

A small crew with a small ship in a system where nobody is coming to help.

The game does not require the player to be moral. It requires the choice to
exist. Every faction can be worked with, robbed, or avoided; nobody is written
as deserving destruction and nobody is written as owed loyalty.

**Smith is in debt. That is the whole motivation.** No prophecy, no revenge, no
lost war. He needs the next job, and the one after that.

## 9. Open questions

- Who does Smith owe, and does it ever come due? (Recommend: yes, once, late.)
- Do the corporate wars progress across a run, or are they static backdrop?
  (Recommend: static in v0.3, reactive in v1.0.)
- Is there an endgame *destination*, or does the run end on completing the
  power armour? A destination gives the map a shape.
